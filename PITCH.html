1 · What you say (≈82 seconds)

0:00
The wedge
Half of UK undergraduates no longer attend all their classes — down from 63% in 2006 to 48% in 2025. It isn't laziness. It's arithmetic. The recording can be paused, rewound, played at 2x. The room can't. Showing up live now buys you less than staying in bed.
0:12
The claim
We're making live attendance mechanically matter again. This is not a note-taking tool. It's a companion artifact that can only be built while you're in the room — because it reacts to you, mid-lecture, while the lecture is still changeable.
0:26
What it is
The room is transcribed live. It streams into a semantic map that grows as the concepts arrive — a radial hierarchy, not a wall of text. Every concept exists at three comprehension levels at once. Intuition: what is this like? Mechanism: how does it work? Rigour: what would a professor grill you on? Switching levels costs zero API calls.
0:48
The bit only live gets you
And you drive it with your voice. Mutter "explain that simpler" and the card you're looking at drops to Intuition. Say "go deeper" and it goes to exam-level Rigour. Each level speaks back in a different voice, so you hear which altitude you're at.
1:04
Why the recording can't do that
A recording can be summarised. It cannot be interrogated at the moment you were confused, by a system that heard the aside the lecturer only said once and never put on a slide. The map you walk out with is proof you were there — and it's better than the map anyone gets from the recording.
1:15
Close
Attendance predicts grades better than any admissions test we have. We stopped arguing with students about attending and gave them a reason to.

Running long? Cut the 0:48 voice-playback sentence and the 1:15 statistic. The wedge and the three levels are the pitch.

3 · The sharpest answer: why is live mechanically better?

“Because the artifact is a function of you being in the room, not of the audio.”

1 · It reacts at the moment of confusion

Say “explain that simpler” while the lecturer is still on that slide and you get the Intuition version before the next concept lands on top of it. Watching a recording, confusion resolves twelve hours later, if at all.

2 · The room has information the recording doesn't

Asides, “this always comes up in the exam”, the answer to another student's question, the thing drawn on the board and never posted. The highest-value 5% of a lecture, systematically the part that doesn't survive to the slides.

3 · Attending now produces the better study asset

That's the incentive flip. Attendance is the best-known predictor of grades (ρ ≈ .44, Credé 2010) and lecture capture has been shown to reduce attendance and attainment (Edwards & Clinton 2019). We're changing what's on each side of the trade.

The honest one-liner

Nothing stops you pointing this at a recording — but then you get a summary. In the room you get a dialogue. Different products, and only one requires you to have been there.

4 · Six questions judges will ask

“Isn't this just Otter or NotebookLM plus a graph?”
Overlap is real. Otter does live transcription; NotebookLM does mind maps. Both are retrospective — you give them a corpus, they return an artifact. Neither is steerable by your voice while the source event is still happening, and neither carries three simultaneous comprehension levels per concept where two are free to switch. Our unit isn't a document, it's a concept node with an altitude dial, built during the event.
“Why won't students just run it on the recording?”
They can, and it'll work — we ship a paste-text box. They get a worse artifact: no voice levelling at the moment of confusion, no asides, no chance to ask the human. The moat is output quality, not technical prevention. Concede this cleanly if pushed twice — defensiveness here costs more than the point.
“What happens when the API quota runs out on stage?”
Partly designed for. Every text call goes through a model-fallback chain (backend/services/llm.py) because separate model names have separate free-tier pools — that has already saved one live run. TTS and Rigour text are cached by concept and served from disk, so anything pre-warmed costs zero calls. Levels 1–2, the whole-map selector, layout and pan/zoom are 100% client-side and cannot be rate-limited. What genuinely dies is fresh extraction — and the answer to that is the recorded backup run, not a claim it can't happen.
“Does this improve learning outcomes?”
We have no outcome data. Zero. It's a one-day build and anyone claiming otherwise is lying to you. What we have is a design leaning on established findings: attendance predicts grades (Credé 2010), retrieval practice beats re-reading (Agarwal 2021), lecture capture displaces attendance (Edwards & Clinton 2019). The honest test is a term-long A/B against a control cohort — the first thing we'd want funding to run.
“The lecturer's voice is in the same mic — doesn't it fire commands on the lecture itself?”
It did; it's fixed, and it's the sharpest bit of engineering in the build. intent.py is a local regex, not an LLM call, because it runs on every committed segment for free. The problem is that the lecture's vocabulary is the trigger vocabulary — “proof”, “intuition”, “deeper”. So a bare topic word never fires: a match needs an addressed command (“explain that simpler”, “go deeper”) or a first/second-person complaint (“I don't get it”, “you lost me”). “We go deeper into this next week” is lecture, and returns None. Residual risk is now the other direction — unanticipated phrasing silently does nothing — which is why the tabs are always one click away.
“Does this scale, or is it one laptop?”
One laptop today, honestly. Every LLM call runs inline on a single event loop — threads deadlock on the demo machine's cert stack, documented and deliberate. Fine for one listener, wrong for a lecture hall. The fix is known (subprocess/async workers), and the per-concept cache already means the second student asking for the same node's Rigour costs nothing. The multiplayer version — one shared map per room, built by everyone present — is the actually interesting product, and it's what the architecture points at, not something we shipped.
Bonus: privacy
Yes, you're recording a lecturer. The ElevenLabs key never reaches the browser — audio goes browser → our websocket → ElevenLabs — but institutional consent is a real deployment blocker, not a solved problem. Say that plainly.

5 · Pre-stage checklist

  • App running, websocket connected, status line green — http://localhost:8010/static/index.html in Chrome, maximised.
  • .env on this laptop has GEMINI_API_KEY and ELEVENLABS_API_KEY.
  • A ~700-character lecture paragraph in the clipboard — the fallback fuel.
  • Backup recording of a good run open in a tab, one click from playing. If Wi-Fi or quota dies, this is the demo.
  • Rigour pre-warmed on your demo card (cached = instant and free). Same for “Read it to me”.
  • Two or three cards already on screen — safer than an empty map; you can still show growth on top.
  • Rehearse the exact voice phrases: “explain that simpler”, “go deeper”, “how does that actually work”, “back to normal”.
  • Notifications off, second monitor mirrored, browser zoom at a size readable from the back row.

6 · Read this before you walk up

1. The wedge is philosophically soft. Nothing technically stops the app being pointed at a recording. Defence is “the live artifact is better”, not “the recording can't”. Concede cleanly.

2. Voice levelling is the best beat and the likeliest to no-op. Strict matcher, fires only on a final segment, so there's a second of lag. Fallback = click the tab. Never retry the phrase.

3. Free-tier quota is a single point of failure and has already died once today. Fresh extraction is the vulnerable path. Pre-warm everything cacheable.

4. The mic path on your own hardware is the last untested link. Verified here: tab audio from a real lecture video → live transcript → map. Not verified: a human speaking into your laptop's mic. Do that once before stage.

5. No learning-outcome evidence and the product is one day old. An honest “no data, here's the experiment” scores better than a hedge.

6. Sponsor framing. ElevenLabs is genuinely load-bearing (Scribe + TTS) — safe to say. Tavily is not wired in. ElevenLabs video is 402-blocked. Never imply either works.
